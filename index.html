I'm a research data analyst at the Slone Epidemiology Center, Boston University, where I work on the Black Women's Health Study (BWHS) — analyzing RNA-seq, tumor pathology, and tissue microarray data to understand how metabolic conditions and medication use shape breast cancer biology and the tumor immune microenvironment, and building the harmonized datasets and tooling the study runs on. I also work with pooled data from the Breast Cancer Research Foundation (BCRF) Health Equity Initiative, and have studied ovarian cancer risk prediction using polygenic risk scores (PRS) and epidemiologic factors in the PLCO and CPS-II cohorts.

What I work on

Three questions I keep returning to.

How do biology and environment shape cancer?

Using molecular, clinical, and epidemiologic data to understand how metabolic health, medications, and other exposures relate to tumor biology and the immune microenvironment.

Can we better predict who is at risk?

Combining genetic, molecular, and epidemiologic information to improve cancer risk prediction and identify meaningful differences across populations.

How can we make complex health data useful?

Developing and applying statistical and computational methods to turn high-dimensional, longitudinal, and multi-source biomedical data into interpretable evidence.

Selected work

Projects, and what they actually involved.

Slone Epidemiology Center
2025 – present

Obesity, diabetes, and breast cancer gene expression

Built reproducible HPC, R, and shell workflows for RNA-seq preprocessing, alignment, gene count generation, differential expression, pathway analysis, and immune profiling — first across 418 BWHS breast cancer cases, then extended to 667 after integrating a second sequencing batch. A large share of the work was quality control: assessing batch effects, testing approaches to reduce their influence, and handling imbalance in metformin exposure groups.

RNA-seq DESeq2 HPC batch effects shell
Slone Epidemiology Center
2025 – present

Hypertension and antihypertensive medication use in tumor gene expression

Constructed pre-diagnosis exposure variables for hypertension history, current and ever ARB use, and other antihypertensive medications, then linked them to BWHS clinical and RNA-seq data. Ran DESeq2 differential expression with demographic and clinical adjustment, ssGSEA and Reactome pathway analysis, and CIBERSORTx immune profiling — overall and stratified by ER status to look for heterogeneity by subtype.

DESeq2 ssGSEA Reactome CIBERSORTx

Molecular subtyping and immune profiling of breast tumors

Integrated PAM50 intrinsic subtypes, P53 mutation status, immunohistochemistry measurements, and tumor pathology variables into unified analysis datasets. Applied consensus clustering to identify immune subtypes and built reproducible R workflows for stratified odds-ratio analyses across ER status and tumor characteristics.

PAM50 consensus clustering immune subtyping
