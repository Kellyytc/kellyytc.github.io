Reading tumors,
cohorts, and risk.

I'm a research data analyst at the Slone Epidemiology Center, Boston University, where I work on the Black Women's Health Study (BWHS) — analyzing RNA-seq, tumor pathology, and tissue microarray data to understand how metabolic conditions and medication use shape breast cancer biology and the tumor immune microenvironment. I have also worked on ovarian cancer risk prediction using polygenic risk scores (PRS) and epidemiologic factors in the PLCO and CPS-II cohorts.

What I work on

Three questions I keep returning to.

How do biology and environment shape cancer?

Using molecular, clinical, and epidemiologic data to understand how metabolic health, medications, and other exposures relate to tumor biology and the immune microenvironment.

Can we better predict who is at risk?

Combining genetic, molecular, and epidemiologic information to improve cancer risk prediction and identify meaningful differences across populations.

How can we make complex health data useful?

Developing and applying statistical and computational methods to turn high-dimensional, longitudinal, and multi-source biomedical data into interpretable evidence.

Spatial tissue microarray workflow & a Shiny platform for BWHS

Built a workflow to quantify and visualize immune and tumor-cell markers across epithelial and stromal compartments. Polygenic risk scores for ovarian cancer risk stratification

Combining PRS with epidemiologic variables in the PLCO screening trial and the CPS-II cohort to test whether genetic risk prediction improves absolute risk estimation beyond established factors, using the iCare package for model development and validation. Also contributing to a scoping review of 600+ ovarian cancer publications with a 10+ member team.
